\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
All numbers are mean $\pm$ standard deviation over three split seeds. Every
choice is made on validation; the chosen models are then refit on training plus
validation and scored once on test. The primary metric is the MAE of $\hat c$
against the reference, in each gas's units, on hours with a reference reading.
The \emph{resolution floor} is the MAE of a perfect bin prediction. Against the
hour-of-day baseline $P(G_j \mid H)$, which sees the same evidence but reads
only the hour, we report
\begin{equation}
  \text{skill} = 1 - \frac{\text{MAE}_\text{model}}{\text{MAE}_\text{hour}},
  \qquad
  \text{gap closed} =
  \frac{\text{MAE}_\text{hour} - \text{MAE}_\text{model}}
       {\text{MAE}_\text{hour} - \text{MAE}_\text{floor}},
  \label{eq:skill}
\end{equation}
and the mean log-likelihood of the true bin (comparable only at equal bin
counts). Results use 3 bins; 5 bins is a sensitivity check.

On held-out test weeks (\cref{tab:test}) the final model beats the hour of day
on every gas, removing 37\%, 32\% and 18\% of the baseline's CO, NO$_x$ and
NO$_2$ error and placing more probability on the true bin. It closes 87\% of
the gap to the floor for CO and 71\% for NO$_x$, so for these gases most of the
remaining error comes from the coarse bins, not the model. NO$_2$ closes only
39\%: its limit is the evidence, since its only nominal sensor responds more to
CO (\cref{sec:learned}). These skills match validation (0.37, 0.34, 0.16);
raw MAEs are higher on test because the floor is too (CO 0.549 vs 0.461).

On CO the final model matches Phase~1 (0.601 vs 0.604~mg/m$^3$, within seed
noise) while also estimating NO$_x$ and NO$_2$, and its posterior is sharper
(log-likelihood $-0.569$ vs $-0.657$). Phase~1 lets every sensor witness CO
directly, which double-counts shared evidence: removing $S_1$ or $S_5$ from its
evidence \emph{raises} validation log-likelihood in every seed.

Five bins lower every MAE (test CO $0.601 \to 0.521$, NO$_x$ $99.9 \to 82.5$,
NO$_2$ $25.6 \to 23.3$), but the floor drops faster (CO $0.549 \to 0.366$), so
the gap closed falls (CO 0.87 to 0.73): finer bins raise the ceiling but leave
fewer training hours per CPT row (\cref{fig:test-mae}).

\Cref{tab:ladder} shows what each addition buys. Tying each sensor only to
its nominal gas (Phase~2) loses CO accuracy, because the other sensors also
carry CO information; the cross-sensitivity edges (Phase~3a) recover it,
lowering MAE relative to Phase~2 by 9.2\%, 6.1\% and 3.4\% for CO, NO$_x$ and
NO$_2$. The latent regime (Phase~3b) then improves the \emph{joint} fit:
validation log-likelihood rises by 0.39 nats per hour over Phase~3a
($+0.29$/$+0.41$/$+0.46$ per seed) with fewer parameters.

The gain comes from where Phase~3a's assumption fails (\cref{fig:b1}): within
a single hour the gases still have a mean rank correlation of 0.54 (0.66
overall), but within a regime only 0.05, and 0.39 when the regime is inferred
from the sensors alone; NO$_x$--NO$_2$ keeps 0.22, as NO$_2$ is part of NO$_x$.
Per-gas error changes much less: relative to Phase~3a, CO and NO$_x$ MAE are
unchanged ($0.003 \pm 0.030$ and $0.016 \pm 0.052$ in skill) and NO$_2$ falls
by about 14\% ($0.137 \pm 0.082$). The sensors already pin down CO and NO$_x$
hour by hour, so the regime mainly helps the gas they read worst; its main
effect is on the relationships \emph{between} gases, which a per-gas error
cannot see.

\subsection{What the Model Learned}
\label{sec:learned}
The three regimes are consistent across seeds (at most 0.08 apart in any
$P(G_j \mid R)$) and interpretable (\cref{fig:regimes}): \emph{quiet} puts every
gas in its lowest bin and dominates 02:00--05:00 on weekdays, \emph{moderate}
covers late evenings and much of the weekend, and \emph{busy} peaks on weekdays
at 08:00--10:00 and 17:00--20:00. The model was never told about commuting.
The cross-sensitivity study (\cref{app:cross}) explains the NO$_2$ result:
$S_4$, nominally an NO$_2$ sensor, tracks CO more strongly than NO$_2$.

The main failure modes come from the discretization and the static CPTs.
Because $\hat c$ mixes bin means, it caps peaks at the top bin's training mean
and overshoots in clean weeks (\cref{app:trace}). One set of CPTs covers the
year: trained on the first half of the year, Phase~2's CO MAE rises from 0.54
to 0.81~mg/m$^3$ on later months and NO$_x$ error more than doubles as winter
levels exceed the training bins. The fourth regime at $K_R = 4$ is seasonal,
which points the same way.

\subsection{Convergence and Stability}
\label{sec:stability}
Every EM run is monotone and converges before its cap: Phase~1 in 3
iterations (CO is observed on 82\% of hours), Phase~3b in at most 57 during
selection and 9--14 in the final run. Validation log-likelihood keeps rising
with $K_R$ ($+0.14$ nats per hour from 2 to 3, $+0.03$ from 3 to 4), but the
fourth regime differs between restarts (state profiles up to 0.60 apart), and
models that keep hour $\to$ gas edges are never reproducible. $K_R = 3$ agrees
within 0.09 in two seeds and 0.15 in the third, which a 20-restart check shows
is the same solution reached imperfectly; we keep it as a flagged judgement
call, with $K_R = 2$ as the strict alternative. Inference stays exact and
linear in the number of hours, but its cost grows as $K^{\#\text{gases}}$
(81 joint states at 3 bins, 375 at 5).
