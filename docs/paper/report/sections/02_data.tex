\section{Data Sourcing and Processing}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Source.} We use the UCI Air Quality dataset
\citep{vito2008airquality, devito2008calibration}: 9,357 hourly averages
(March 2004 to April 2005) from one road-level device in an Italian city, with
five metal-oxide sensors ($S_1$--$S_5$), temperature, relative and absolute
humidity, and reference CO, NMHC, C$_6$H$_6$, NO$_x$ and NO$_2$ from a
co-located analyzer. \Cref{tab:variables} (\cref{app:variables}) lists every
variable, its role and whether we keep it.

\paragraph{Missing data.} Missing readings ($-200$ in the raw file) are
recoded as missing. Sensors and weather drop out together during device
outages on 3.9\% of hours; we drop those 366 hours (8,991 remain), since
inputs have no CPT and cannot be inferred. The references are missing far more
often (CO 18.0\%, NO$_x$ and NO$_2$ 17.5\%), in two patterns: a nightly
dropout, consistent with scheduled calibration (93\% of 3\,AM NO$_x$ and
NO$_2$ readings, \cref{fig:missing-hour}), and multi-day analyzer outages,
which hold 77--84\% of the missing hours. The first depends on the hour, which
the model conditions on, and during the second the sensors read much as usual,
so we treat the gaps as missing at random given the observed variables
\citep{rubin1976inference}; EM uses every retained hour.

\paragraph{Variable selection.} NMHC is observed on only 914 hours, almost all
in spring 2004, so it is dropped. C$_6$H$_6$ is an almost exact function of
$S_2$ (cubic fit $R^2 \approx 0.99998$) and carries no information beyond it
(\cref{app:cross}), so it is dropped too, and with it $S_2$, which has no gas
parent left. Weather is $W = (T, \mathit{RH})$; absolute humidity is mostly
determined by the two and would triple every sensor CPT.

\paragraph{Discretization and splits.} Gases, sensors, T and RH are cut into
$K$ quantile bins fit on the training data only ($K = 3$ by default, $K = 5$
as a check); each gas bin keeps the mean concentration of its training hours,
which turns a posterior back into a concentration. Instead of the proposal's
single chronological split, we cut the hours into 7-day blocks, shuffle them
and assign them 50/25/25 to training, validation and test, for three seeds.
Neighbouring hours are near-duplicates (residual lag-1 autocorrelation
0.68--0.88), so whole weeks keep copies of test hours out of training while
covering the whole year. Validation makes every choice; test is scored once.

\paragraph{Limitations.} One device, one site and one year: held-out weeks
measure interpolation within that year, not transfer, and the reference is
treated as ground truth. Drift is not modeled; on a chronological split the
error grows on later months (\cref{sec:learned}).
