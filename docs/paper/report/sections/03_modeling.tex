\subsection{Bayesian Network}
\label{sec:bn}

\paragraph{Variables.} Each hour $t$ is described by the hour of day
$H \in \{0,\dots,23\}$, a weekday indicator $D \in \{0,1\}$ (used only by the
latent regime, \cref{sec:latent}), the weather $W = (T, \mathit{RH})$, the
reference gases $G = (G_{\mathrm{CO}}, G_{\mathrm{NO_x}}, G_{\mathrm{NO_2}})$
and the sensors $S = (S_1, S_3, S_4, S_5)$. Every continuous variable is
discretized into $K$ quantile bins fit on the training split (\cref{sec:data});
$K = 3$ unless stated. The gases are partially observed in training and always
hidden at prediction time; $H$, $D$ and $W$ are always observed.

\paragraph{Structure.} Without the latent regime, the network factorizes as
\begin{equation}
  P(G, S \mid H, W) = \prod_{j} P(G_j \mid H)
  \prod_{i} P\bigl(S_i \mid \mathrm{Pa}_G(S_i), T, \mathit{RH}\bigr),
  \label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Pa}_G(S_i)$ are the gases sensor $i$ responds to. The inputs have
no CPTs; we only condition on them. Hour $\to$ gas captures the daily traffic
cycle; gas $\to$ sensor links each sensor to its nominal target
\citep{devito2008calibration} ($S_1$: CO, $S_3$: NO$_x$, $S_4$: NO$_2$); and
weather $\to$ sensor reflects the metal-oxide sensors' dependence on
temperature and humidity. A cross-sensitivity study (\cref{app:cross}) added
CO and NO$_x$ as parents of $S_4$, beyond its nominal NO$_2$, and of $S_5$,
whose nominal target O$_3$ has no reference. NMHC (90\% missing), C$_6$H$_6$
(an almost exact function of $S_2$) and $S_2$ itself are excluded. Phase~1 is
the single-gas case of \cref{eq:factorization}, with CO as the only gas
(\cref{app:networks}).

\paragraph{Assumptions.} \Cref{eq:factorization} assumes that the gases are
independent given the hour, that each sensor is independent of the others
given its parents, and that hours are independent with one set of CPTs for the
whole year. The first is the strongest, since all three gases come from
traffic; \cref{sec:latent} relaxes it. The second can make the posterior
overconfident when several sensors share a signal the model does not include.
The third ignores autocorrelation and sensor drift.

\paragraph{Parameters.} A node with $K_X$ states and parent configurations
$\prod K_U$ has $(K_X - 1)\prod K_U$ free parameters. At $K = 3$,
\cref{eq:factorization} has 900, and $S_4$, with five parents, accounts for
486 of them.

\subsection{Latent Emission Regime}
\label{sec:latent}
\todo{Muska check this section}
\Cref{eq:factorization} treats the three gases as independent given the hour,
but they share one source, traffic, and still rise and fall together within a
given hour (\cref{sec:results}). We therefore add a latent emission regime
$R \in \{1,\dots,K_R\}$ between the time inputs and the gases, and replace
$\prod_j P(G_j \mid H)$ with
\begin{equation}
  P(G \mid H, D) = \sum_{r=1}^{K_R} P(R{=}r \mid H, D)
  \prod_{j} P(G_j \mid R{=}r).
  \label{eq:latent}
\end{equation}
$R$ has no column and is hidden on every hour, so its CPTs are learned only
through EM (\cref{sec:em}). Because the gases depend on time only through $R$,
the weekday indicator $D$ enters once, in $P(R \mid H, D)$, rather than in a
table per gas. At $K_R = 3$ the regime and gas CPTs have 114 free parameters,
against 144 for the three $P(G_j \mid H)$ tables, so the full network has 870
rather than 900.

If $R$ captures the shared cause, the gases should be nearly independent within
a regime. We test this on validation hours where both gases of a pair are
observed: each hour is soft-assigned to the regimes by its posterior over $R$,
and we compute the posterior-weighted gas--gas rank correlation within each
regime. We infer $R$ two ways: from all observed values, and from the sensors
alone, which is stricter because the posterior cannot use the gas readings
being tested.

We compared $K_R \in \{2,3,4\}$, with weekday or day of week as the day
variable, by mean validation log-likelihood per hour over three split seeds,
preferring configurations whose regimes are reproducible across restarts
(\cref{sec:em}). Adding a direct $H \to G_j$ edge alongside $R$ never gave a
reproducible solution, so we keep $R$ as the gases' only parent.
\Cref{sec:results} discusses the choice of $K_R = 3$ with $R \leftarrow (H, D)$.

\subsection{Learning with Missing Data (EM)}
\label{sec:em}

\paragraph{What is hidden.} During training the reference gases are missing on
17--18\% of hours (\cref{sec:data}), and the latent regime $R$ is missing on
every hour. Because the reference gaps depend on the hour, which the model
conditions on, rather than on the concentration itself, we treat them as
missing at random.

\paragraph{EM.} Rather than imputing missing values, EM averages over them. In
the E-step, each training hour contributes expected counts
$\hat N(x,u) = \sum_t P(X{=}x, U{=}u \mid e_t; \theta)$ to every family
$(X, U)$: an indicator where the family is observed, and the current posterior
over the missing values where it is not. The M-step re-estimates each CPT row
from these counts with Laplace smoothing ($\alpha = 1$):
\begin{equation}
  \theta_{x \mid u} = \frac{\hat N(x,u) + \alpha}{\sum_{x'} \hat N(x',u) + K_X \alpha}.
\end{equation}
This is MAP estimation under a Dirichlet prior, so EM is guaranteed never to
decrease the objective $\mathcal{L}$, the log-likelihood plus log-prior.

\paragraph{Initialization and convergence.} CPTs of nodes with data start from
smoothed counts on the hours where their family is fully observed; the latent
regime $R$ starts from random tables drawn with a fixed seed. EM stops when an
iteration improves $\mathcal{L}$ by less than a relative tolerance,
$\mathcal{L}^{(s)} - \mathcal{L}^{(s-1)} < \epsilon\,\lvert \mathcal{L}^{(s-1)} \rvert$
with $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$, or after 100 iterations. We record $\mathcal{L}$ at
every iteration and check that it never decreases beyond round-off; a decrease
would indicate an implementation error. Phase~1 converges in 3 iterations
because CO is observed on 82\% of hours.

\paragraph{Stability of the latent regime.} With $R$ fully hidden, the
objective has several local optima, and the same regimes can come out in a
different order on each run (label switching). We run several restarts, match
states across runs by their gas profiles, and accept a
number of regimes $K_R$ only if the matched states agree across restarts. That
way the regimes mean the same thing every time we fit and can be interpreted
(\cref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}
At prediction time the evidence is $e_t = (H, D, W, S)$; the reference gases
are never evidence. With the latent regime the hidden variables of an hour are
$(R, G_{\mathrm{CO}}, G_{\mathrm{NO_x}}, G_{\mathrm{NO_2}})$, only
$K_R K^3 = 81$ joint states at $K = K_R = 3$, so we compute every posterior
exactly by enumeration:
\begin{equation}
  P(G_j \mid e_t) \propto \sum_{r} \sum_{g_{-j}}
  P(r \mid H, D) \prod_{k} P(g_k \mid r)
  \prod_{s} P\bigl(s \mid \mathrm{Pa}_G(s), T, \mathit{RH}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Without the regime, the sum over $r$ is replaced by $\prod_k P(g_k \mid H)$.
The cost is linear in the number of hours, and the same sums give the E-step's
expected counts. The concentration estimate is the posterior mean over bins,
$\hat c_{j,t} = \sum_b P(G_j{=}b \mid e_t)\,\bar c_{j,b}$, where $\bar c_{j,b}$
is the training mean of bin $b$. Because $\hat c_{j,t}$ can only mix the $K$
bin means, its error has a floor even with a perfect posterior; we report that
resolution floor with every MAE (\cref{sec:results}).
