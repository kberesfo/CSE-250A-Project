\subsection{Bayesian Network}
\label{sec:bn}

Each hour has the hour of day $H \in \{0,\dots,23\}$, a weekday indicator
$D \in \{0,1\}$, weather $W = (T, \mathit{RH})$, reference gases
$G = (G_{\mathrm{CO}}, G_{\mathrm{NO_x}}, G_{\mathrm{NO_2}})$ and sensors
$S = (S_1, S_3, S_4, S_5)$, each discretized into $K$ bins (\cref{sec:data}).
$H$, $D$ and $W$ are always observed and have no CPTs; the gases are partially
observed in training and always hidden at prediction time. Without the latent
regime the network factorizes as
\begin{equation}
  P(G, S \mid H, W) = \prod_{j} P(G_j \mid H)
  \prod_{i} P\bigl(S_i \mid \mathrm{Pa}_G(S_i), T, \mathit{RH}\bigr),
  \label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Pa}_G(S_i)$ are the gases sensor $i$ responds to. Hour $\to$ gas
captures the daily traffic cycle; weather $\to$ sensor reflects the
metal-oxide sensors' dependence on temperature and humidity; and each sensor
has its nominal target \citep{devito2008calibration} ($S_1$: CO, $S_3$:
NO$_x$, $S_4$: NO$_2$) plus the cross-sensitivities selected from data
(\cref{app:cross}): CO and NO$_x$ for $S_4$, and for $S_5$, whose target O$_3$
has no reference. Phase~1 is the single-gas case with CO only
(\cref{app:networks}). \Cref{eq:factorization} assumes the gases are
independent given the hour, each sensor is independent of the others given its
parents, and hours are independent with one set of CPTs for the year. The
first is the strongest, since all three gases come from traffic, and
\cref{sec:latent} relaxes it; the second can make the posterior overconfident
when sensors share an unmodeled signal.

\subsection{Latent Emission Regime}
\label{sec:latent}
The three gases share one source, traffic, and still co-move within a given
hour (\cref{sec:q2}). We therefore add a latent regime
$R \in \{1,\dots,K_R\}$ and replace $\prod_j P(G_j \mid H)$ with
\begin{equation}
  P(G \mid H, D) = \sum_{r=1}^{K_R} P(R{=}r \mid H, D)
  \prod_{j} P(G_j \mid R{=}r).
  \label{eq:latent}
\end{equation}
$R$ is hidden on every hour, so its CPTs are learned only through EM. The
gases depend on time only through $R$, so $D$ enters once rather than in a
table per gas, and the network is smaller: 870 free parameters at
$K = K_R = 3$ against 900 without the regime (486 of them in $S_4$'s CPT). If
$R$ captures the shared cause, the gases should be nearly independent within a
regime; we test this with posterior-weighted rank correlations, inferring $R$
either from all observed values or, more strictly, from the sensors alone. We
compared $K_R \in \{2,3,4\}$ and weekday vs.\ day of week by validation
log-likelihood, requiring regimes that are reproducible across restarts;
keeping a direct $H \to G_j$ edge alongside $R$ was never reproducible.

\subsection{Learning with Missing Data (EM)}
\label{sec:em}
EM averages over the missing references and the latent regime rather than
imputing them. In the E-step each training hour contributes expected counts
$\hat N(x,u) = \sum_t P(X{=}x, U{=}u \mid e_t; \theta)$ to every family
$(X, U)$: an indicator where the family is observed, the current posterior
where it is not. The M-step re-estimates each CPT row with Laplace smoothing
($\alpha = 1$),
\begin{equation}
  \theta_{x \mid u} = \frac{\hat N(x,u) + \alpha}{\sum_{x'} \hat N(x',u) + K_X \alpha},
\end{equation}
which is MAP estimation under a Dirichlet prior (\cref{app:em}), so EM never
decreases the objective $\mathcal{L}$ (log-likelihood plus log-prior). CPTs
start from smoothed complete-case counts and $R$ from seeded random tables. EM
stops when $\mathcal{L}$ improves by less than $10^{-4}\lvert\mathcal{L}\rvert$
or after 100 iterations (200 with the regime); we check that $\mathcal{L}$
never decreases. With $R$ hidden, the objective has local optima and the
regimes can come out in a different order on each run, so we run five restarts,
keep the best training objective, match states across runs by their gas
profiles, and accept $K_R$ only if the matched states agree. The final test
models use the same procedure on training plus validation, never on test.

\subsection{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}
At prediction time the evidence is $e_t = (H, D, W, S)$, never a reference
reading. The hidden variables of an hour are $(R, G)$, only $K_R K^3 = 81$
joint states at 3 bins, so every posterior is exact by enumeration:
\begin{equation}
  P(G_j \mid e_t) \propto \sum_{r} \sum_{g_{-j}}
  P(r \mid H, D) \prod_{k} P(g_k \mid r)
  \prod_{s} P\bigl(s \mid \mathrm{Pa}_G(s), T, \mathit{RH}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The same sums give the E-step's expected counts, at a cost linear in the
number of hours. The concentration estimate is the posterior mean over bins,
$\hat c_{j,t} = \sum_b P(G_j{=}b \mid e_t)\,\bar c_{j,b}$, with $\bar c_{j,b}$
the mean concentration of training hours in bin $b$. It can only mix the $K$
bin means, so its error has a floor even with a perfect posterior, which we
report with every MAE.
