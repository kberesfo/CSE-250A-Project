\paragraph{Q1 (estimation).} On the test set, the final model (Phase~3b) beats the hour-of-day baseline on every gas, reducing
concentration error (MAE) by 37\% for CO, 32\% for NO$_x$ and 18\%
for NO$_2$, and matches the single-gas network (Phase~1) on CO while covering three gases (\cref{sec:results}).
CO and NO$_x$ come close to the 3-bin floor, but NO$_2$ does not because
its own sensor tracks CO more than NO$_2$.

\paragraph{Q2 (shared structure).} On validation, the latent regime explains
most of the co-movement between gases, lowering their mean rank correlation
to 0.05 within a regime, and improves the joint fit
over the no-regime ablation (Phase~3a) (\cref{sec:q2}).
Per-gas error barely changes; the regime fixes the relationships between gases, which per-gas metrics
cannot see.

\paragraph{Chain of experiments.} Each phase changed one modeling choice and kept the rest fixed. Phase~1, a single CO
network with every sensor as its child, is accurate but overconfident because it
double-counts the sensors' shared signal. Phase~2 adds NO$_x$ and NO$_2$ and ties each
sensor only to its own gas, so CO loses accuracy with only one sensor to read.
Phase~3a adds the cross-sensitivity edges chosen by our sensor study (\cref{app:cross}) and recovers
it, and Phase~3b adds the latent regime, which captures the joint structure between gases.
Each phase's independence assumptions are only approximately true, yet they keep the network 
small enough to learn from one year of data, and the final network still improves on the 
hour of day for every gas.

\paragraph{Usefulness.} The network returns a posterior over each gas's level, which we turn into a concentration
in real units, so a deployed device that sees only the hour, weather and its own sensors
can report CO, NO$_x$ and NO$_2$ estimates with their uncertainty. A reference analyzer is
needed only for training, and for CO and NO$_x$ the estimates already come close
to the limit the bins allow, which makes the model a practical way to
calibrate low-cost sensors where reference stations are too expensive.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Hours are treated as independent and the CPTs are stationary;
seasonal drift is a real cost. On a chronological split, Phase~2's
errors rise sharply (\cref{sec:learned}).
Error is also bounded by the 3-bin resolution floor. The data come from one device, site and year.
Reference gaps are assumed missing at random \citep{rubin1976inference}. $K_R = 3$ is a judgement call.
C$_6$H$_6$ is a transform of $S_2$ and NMHC is 90\% missing, so both are excluded.

\paragraph{Future work.} The next step is a temporal regime, a Markov
chain $P(R_t \given R_{t-1})$ learned with Baum--Welch \citep{rabiner1989hmm},
to use the strong hour-to-hour dependence. A season signal could 
capture seasonal effects, and more bins with 
stronger priors would lower the resolution floor.
