\subsection{Findings}

\paragraph{Q1 (estimation).} On the test set, the final model (Phase~3b) beats the hour-of-day baseline on every gas, reducing
concentration error (MAE) by 37\% for CO, 32\% for NO$_x$ and 18\%
for NO$_2$ (\cref{sec:results}), and on CO it matches the single-gas network
(Phase~1) with an MAE of 0.60~mg/m$^3$ while putting more probability on the true CO bin and covering three gases.
For CO and NO$_x$, it closes 87\% and 71\% of the gap to the 3-bin resolution floor,
which means the bins now limit accuracy; for NO$_2$, however, it closes only 39\% because its own sensor
tracks CO more than NO$_2$.

\paragraph{Q2 (shared structure).} On validation, the latent regime explains
most of the co-movement between gases, lowering their mean rank correlation
from 0.54 within an hour of day to 0.05 within a regime and to 0.39 when the
regime is inferred from the sensors alone, and it improves the joint fit by
0.39 nats per hour over the no-regime ablation (Phase~3a). 
Per-gas error barely changes, apart from a 14\% drop for NO$_2$
with wide uncertainty, because the sensors already pin down each gas; the
regime fixes the relationships between gases, which per-gas metrics
cannot see.

Across the ablations, Phase~1 is accurate but overconfident because it
double-counts the sensors' shared signal; nominal edges (Phase~2) are
calibrated but lose CO accuracy; cross-sensitivity edges (Phase~3a) recover
it; and the regime (Phase~3b) adds the joint structure.

\subsection{Limitations} Hours are treated as independent, so there is no temporal
model. CPTs are stationary, so seasonal drift is ignored and year-round bin
means overshoot in clean seasons. The resolution floor at 3 bins bounds the
achievable error. All data comes from one device at one site over one year.
Reference gaps are assumed missing at random \citep{rubin1976inference}. The
choice of $K_R = 3$ regimes is a judgement call since one seed narrowly misses our
stability rule. Since C$_6$H$_6$ is a transform of $S_2$ and NMHC is about 90\%
missing, both are excluded.

\paragraph{Future work.} The natural next step is a temporal regime, a Markov
chain $P(R_t \given R_{t-1})$ learned with Baum--Welch \citep{rabiner1989hmm},
which would use the strong hour-to-hour dependence we observe. A season signal, 
either as a parent of the gases or through season-aware bin means, could 
capture the seasonal state that appears at $K_R = 4$, and more bins with 
stronger priors would lower the resolution floor.
